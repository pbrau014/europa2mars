EUROPA-TO-MARS TRANSFORMATION PROJECT
Revised Conceptual Proposal — Comprehensive, Text-Document Format
Project Overview
The EUROPA-TO-MARS TRANSFORMATION PROJECT proposes relocating Europa, one of Jupiter's moons, to Mars, with the objective of introducing substantial water, ice, minerals, volatile compounds and other physical resources into the Martian environment. The project assumes the development of hypothetical technologies capable of reducing Europa's effective mass during transportation and altering Mars's effective gravity to help improve the retention of atmospheric gases and water.

This revised version incorporates the change from a controlled, staged material-delivery concept to a collision-based delivery assumption, while distinguishing the transportation phase, the collision aftermath, environmental development, water stabilization and biological growth. The original Scenario A and Scenario B timelines are retained as conditional planning assumptions, not established predictions.

This proposal is speculative. The mass-reduction technology, gravity-control technology, relocation of Europa, collision outcome and associated timelines have not been demonstrated. The 150-year transportation period remains a project target rather than a validated trajectory or propulsion calculation.

PART 1 — SHARED PROJECT FOUNDATION
1. Overall Objective
The primary objective is to move Europa from its original orbital environment around Jupiter to Mars and introduce its physical inventory into the Martian environment. The proposal aims to use this material, in combination with a hypothetical gravity-control system, to help develop environmental conditions more favorable to persistent water and eventually plant life.

The project is divided into two broad operations:
1. Europa transportation and delivery: Relocate Europa from the Jupiter orbital region toward Mars over a proposed 150-year period.
2. Mars environmental and biological development: After delivery, assess the resulting environment, develop conditions for water stability, prepare growing regions and work toward initial biological growth.
The proposed transportation period begins when Europa's relocation first starts and ends at the project's defined delivery endpoint. The post-delivery development clock is intended to begin only after the delivery phase has been completed.

The two scenarios share the transportation concept and differ in their anticipated post-delivery development periods.
- Scenario A: 150 years of transportation and delivery, followed by up to 150 years of environmental and biological development.
- Scenario B: 150 years of transportation and delivery, followed by an original accelerated target of 10–18 months for atmospheric and surface stabilization, initial water stability and first observable plant growth.
The revised collision-based assumption introduces a major uncertainty between delivery and environmental development. The collision itself cannot be treated as proof that the water, atmosphere or surface conditions will immediately be suitable for the next stages.
2. Hypothetical Europa Mass-Reduction System
The transportation concept assumes a hypothetical microwave-field system surrounding Europa that reduces its effective mass by 99.9999%. The purpose of this assumption is to make the proposed relocation concept more manageable within the project's hypothetical transportation framework.

Europa's approximate natural mass is:
\[
M_{\mathrm{natural}}=4.798\times10^{22}\ \mathrm{kg}
\]

A 99.9999% reduction would leave one-millionth of the original effective mass, or a fraction of \(10^{-6}\).

The calculation is:
\[
M_{\mathrm{effective}}
=4.798\times10^{22}\times10^{-6}
\]

\[
\boxed{M_{\mathrm{effective}}=4.798\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{kg}}
\]

The arithmetic is correct under the stipulated assumption.

This calculation does not demonstrate that such a field is physically possible. Present-day microwave technology is not known to reduce the inertial or gravitational mass of a moon.

The proposal also assumes that the physical inventory of Europa remains available despite the hypothetical effective-mass reduction. This inventory includes:
- Water and ice.
- Rock and minerals.
- Salts and other compounds.
- Volatile materials.
- Other potentially useful physical resources.
The assumed reduction in effective mass does not, within the proposal, mean that the physical inventory of water, rock or other materials disappears.

An unresolved fundamental question is whether the hypothetical field would alter inertial mass, gravitational mass, or both. That distinction would affect the theoretical behavior of Europa during transportation and would need to be resolved before the concept could be treated as an engineering model.
3. Europa Transportation and Propulsion Concept
The proposal uses SpaceX Raptor engines as a reference propulsion technology for the conceptual relocation system. The transportation concept assumes an engine arrangement around Europa's middle, with Europa maintaining a fixed orientation during the modeled operation.

The working assumptions are:
- A propulsion arrangement is positioned around the middle of Europa.
- Europa maintains a fixed orientation during the modeled operation.
- Europa does not rotate or spin during the modeled operation.
- Engines are assumed to operate at full capacity.
- Fuel and propellant calculations are excluded from the conceptual scenario as previously stipulated.
- The overall transportation period remains a proposed 150 years.
These assumptions are not equivalent to an engineered propulsion design. The number of engines, total thrust, structural arrangement, loads, trajectory, acceleration, deceleration, departure, arrival and braking have not been validated.

An earlier estimate of 23,134 engines is retained only as an unverified preliminary figure. It is not a confirmed engine count or an established design requirement.

The proposed transportation system would ultimately require a detailed assessment of the propulsion arrangement, the hypothetical mass-reduction mechanism, structural behavior, trajectory and orbital dynamics. The 150-year schedule cannot be confirmed without that work.
4. Europa's Journey Toward Mars and the Asteroid-Belt Region
The proposal retains a 150-year transportation period as its target schedule. However, Europa's relocation would involve a changing orbital environment rather than a simple straight-line movement from Jupiter to Mars.

The transportation concept must account for the orbital positions of the relevant bodies, gravitational influences, trajectory evolution and arrival conditions.

The asteroid belt is explicitly included as a region that the transportation assessment must consider. It is not a solid wall of asteroids, but the region contains objects whose orbital paths and gravitational influences must be taken into account when assessing a large-object relocation concept.

The transportation assessment would need to address:
- The initial orbital conditions.
- Departure from Europa's original orbital environment.
- The overall transfer toward Mars.
- The asteroid-belt region.
- Continued inward transfer.
- Mars's changing orbital position.
- Arrival conditions.
- The transportation and arrival uncertainties.
The project does not currently have a validated route, crossing duration or collision-risk model.

The 150-year duration is a planning constraint. It is not derived from a verified trajectory, engine arrangement or complete orbital calculation.
5. Revised Delivery Assumption — Collision-Based Delivery
The revised proposal assumes that Europa ultimately reaches Mars as part of a collision-based delivery concept rather than the previous controlled, staged delivery of separate materials.

The intended conceptual outcome is to introduce Europa's physical inventory into the Martian environment. The collision is expected to change the environment through the introduction of water-bearing material and other physical resources.

However, the collision does not guarantee that the material will be retained, that water will be distributed evenly, or that the resulting atmosphere and surface will become suitable for biological development.

A planetary-scale collision could involve extreme heating, vaporization, melting, shock effects, debris redistribution, surface alteration, atmospheric changes and potentially substantial material loss.

The outcome would depend on physical conditions that have not been modeled in this proposal. The project therefore separates the collision from the subsequent environmental-development stages.

The revised decision: Treat the collision as a material-introduction event and the post-collision environment as a separate, uncertain development phase.
6. Hypothetical Mars Gravity-Control Field
The proposal assumes a second hypothetical technology: a gravity-control system capable of increasing or regulating effective gravity in selected regions or around Mars in a manner intended to influence atmospheric and water retention.

The intended objectives include helping Mars retain:
- Atmospheric gases.
- Water vapor.
- Surface water.
The proposal discusses an Earth-like effective gravity of approximately 1 g in selected regions as a possible planning reference. This is not a demonstrated requirement, a proven solution or a validated optimum for Mars's environmental development.

The hypothetical field is intended to influence effective gravity in the surface and atmospheric regions relevant to the proposal. However, changing effective gravity is not the same as increasing Mars's physical mass

PART 2 — THE EUROPA TRANSPORTATION AND DELIVERY PHASE
8. The Proposed 150-Year Transportation Period
The project assigns 150 years to the relocation and delivery phase. This duration is intended to provide a long-term framework within which the relocation concept can be evaluated, rather than assuming that the movement of Europa can occur quickly.

The 150-year interval is divided conceptually into several major periods of activity. These include the initial preparation and departure, the long-distance transportation period, consideration of the asteroid-belt region, continued transit toward Mars, and the eventual delivery and post-delivery assessment.

The proposal does not currently establish the exact duration of each individual transportation phase. The phases are organizational milestones rather than independently calculated schedules.

8.1 Initial Preparation and Departure
The initial period is intended to establish the conditions required for the hypothetical transportation system to begin operating. The proposal assumes the mass-reduction system and propulsion system are available and that Europa's relocation can begin.

The departure period would be the beginning of the project's 150-year transportation clock.

The purpose of this milestone is to distinguish the initial movement from the subsequent long-distance transportation period. It also makes clear that the project's time allowance begins with the proposed relocation itself, rather than beginning only after Europa has already completed an unspecified preliminary movement.

The precise duration of this initial phase remains undetermined.

8.2 Long-Distance Transportation
After departure, the project assumes that Europa continues its journey toward the Mars region.

This period represents the major portion of the proposed transportation timeline. It would require the transportation system to remain operational, the hypothetical mass-reduction effect to remain valid, and the movement of Europa to be managed under changing orbital conditions.

The project currently treats the 150-year period as a fixed planning target. However, it has not established a scientifically validated transportation schedule that demonstrates how the proposed system would accomplish the relocation within that period.

The duration is therefore an assumed project constraint, not a calculated result.

8.3 Consideration of the Asteroid-Belt Region
The asteroid-belt region is explicitly included in the transportation assessment because the proposed relocation would involve a changing orbital environment between the outer and inner regions of the Solar System.

The asteroid belt is not uniformly filled with objects. Nevertheless, the region contains numerous bodies with different orbital paths, and its environment would need to be considered in a large-object relocation concept.

The proposal does not establish a particular crossing date, route or duration through the asteroid-belt region. Those values remain unresolved.

The inclusion of this region in the project is intended to prevent the transportation concept from treating the journey as though the space between Jupiter and Mars were a featureless, straight-line corridor.

8.4 Continued Transportation Toward Mars
Following the broader transportation assessment, the project continues to treat the remaining movement toward Mars as part of the same 150-year planning period.

This stage is intended to represent the transition from the long-distance relocation concept toward the eventual delivery phase.

The project would need to establish that the transportation system, the destination conditions and the overall movement assumptions are compatible with the intended arrival outcome.

The precise timing of this stage has not been calculated.

8.5 Arrival and Delivery Milestone
The final transportation milestone is defined as the completion of Europa's planned delivery to Mars.

In the revised concept, delivery is associated with the collision-based assumption rather than the previous staged introduction of individual materials.

The end of the transportation phase is therefore not simply the arrival of Europa in the vicinity of Mars. It is the project's defined delivery endpoint.

The post-delivery development period begins after that endpoint, once the delivery event has occurred and the resulting environment can be evaluated.

The 150-year schedule does not establish that Europa can physically be relocated or that a collision outcome can be achieved within the proposed interval. It remains an assumed timeline requiring independent scientific and engineering assessment.

PART 3 — THE COLLISION AFTERMATH AND ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENT
9. Post-Collision Assessment
The revised proposal treats the collision as a major environmental event that must be assessed separately from the later development of water and plant growth.

The introduction of Europa's material would not necessarily produce a stable Martian environment immediately. The physical consequences could include substantial heating, changes in atmospheric composition, vaporization, melting, redistribution of material, surface alteration and material escaping the planetary environment.

The project's next milestone is therefore a post-collision assessment rather than an immediate assumption that the planet is ready for water stabilization or plant introduction.

This assessment is intended to determine whether the resulting conditions are compatible with the project's subsequent environmental-development goals.

9.1 Environmental Assessment
The post-collision assessment would examine the resulting Martian environment, including:

Atmospheric composition and pressure.

Atmospheric temperature and changes in temperature.

Distribution of water vapor.

Surface conditions.

The physical state and distribution of water-bearing material.

Environmental stability over time.

The extent to which the resulting environment differs from the conditions required for the project's later stages.

The purpose of this assessment is to establish what conditions actually exist after the collision rather than relying entirely on the project's original assumptions.

9.2 Material Retention
The project assumes that Europa's material could contribute water and other useful compounds to Mars. However, the amount of material retained cannot be assumed to equal the amount originally present in Europa.

Some material could become incorporated into the Martian environment, while other material could undergo physical changes or be lost from the environment.

The project therefore distinguishes between Europa's original material inventory and the material that is ultimately retained in a useful form.

The amount of retained water and the resulting environmental conditions remain unknown.

9.3 Atmospheric Changes
The introduction of material could alter the Martian atmosphere, but the direction, magnitude and persistence of those changes have not been established in this proposal.

12. Stage A1 — Post-Delivery Environmental Assessment and Atmospheric Stabilization
Earlier working estimate: 20–50 years for atmospheric and surface stabilization. This is an assumed planning range, not a scientifically calculated prediction.

After Europa's delivery, the first major objective is to assess and progressively develop the resulting atmospheric and surface conditions.

Under the revised collision assumption, this stage cannot simply begin with the expectation that the atmosphere is already ready for stabilization. The collision aftermath itself may involve a period of substantial environmental change.

The assessment must establish the actual state of Mars following the event.

12.1 Establishing the Initial Environmental Conditions
The first step is to determine what the collision has actually produced.

The project would need to assess atmospheric composition, temperature, pressure, water vapor, surface conditions and the distribution of delivered material.

This information would establish the starting point for post-delivery development.

The original proposal assumes that the gravity-control field is already prepared and commissioned, but its actual influence would have to be evaluated against the resulting environmental conditions.

12.2 Atmospheric Monitoring
Atmospheric monitoring is intended to track changes in the Martian atmosphere over time.

The project would assess:

Atmospheric pressure.

Atmospheric composition.

Temperature.

Water vapor.

Atmospheric loss.

Changes associated with the delivery event.

The stability of atmospheric conditions.

These measurements would establish whether the environment is progressing toward the project's intended conditions.

12.3 Surface Monitoring
Surface monitoring would complement atmospheric measurements.

The project would evaluate the condition of the surface, the distribution of delivered material, changes in surface temperature and the areas potentially suitable for later water development.

The purpose is to identify locations where the environmental conditions may eventually permit persistent water and biological preparation.

12.4 Gravity-Control System Assessment
The hypothetical gravity-control field is assumed to be available during this stage.

The project would evaluate whether the field is functioning according to its assumed purpose and whether its influence is associated with changes in atmospheric or water retention.

This is a speculative technological assumption. There is no demonstrated planetary gravity-control system capable of achieving the effects described.

12.5 Stabilization Milestone
The stabilization milestone is not defined solely by the passage of 20–50 years.

The intended milestone would be based on measurable environmental criteria established by the project.

These criteria would include whether selected atmospheric and surface conditions are sufficiently stable for subsequent water-development work.

The end of this stage does not mean that Mars has achieved a complete climate equilibrium. Atmospheric and climate evolution could continue well beyond the stabilization milestone.

13. Stage A2 — Water Accumulation and Stabilization
Earlier working estimate: 30–100 years. This is an assumed planning range, not a calculated water-formation timeline.

The second major post-delivery objective is to establish persistent water in selected Martian regions.

The proposal assumes that Europa introduces a substantial water-bearing inventory into Mars's environment. However, the amount of water retained following the collision and the distribution of that water remain unknown.

Water development is therefore treated as a separate process.

13.1 Water Inventory Assessment
The project would first establish how much water-bearing material remains in the Martian environment after the delivery event.

The original amount of water contained in Europa cannot automatically be equated with the amount that will be available on Mars.

Material may undergo changes in physical state, redistribution or loss.

The water inventory assessment would establish the material available for subsequent environmental development.

13.2 Water Vapor and Condensation
The project assumes that water-bearing material could contribute to atmospheric water vapor and subsequent condensation under suitable conditions.

The development of water systems depends on environmental factors such as temperature, pressure and the physical conditions of the surface.

The proposal does not assume that condensation alone guarantees stable liquid water.

13.3 Establishing Persistent Water
The intended goal is to establish water that can remain available in selected regions rather than merely introducing water into the planetary environment.

The project would assess whether water remains stable over time and whether the resulting conditions support the intended development objectives.

The physical state of water may vary according to local environmental conditions.

13.4 Water Development Milestones
The proposal identifies possible water-development outcomes, including:

Initial localized water accumulation.

More persistent water collections.

Potential larger water bodies.

Possible connected water systems.

Potentially extensive water bodies if the material inventory and environmental conditions permit.

These are potential outcomes, not guaranteed stages. The project does not establish that rivers, lakes or oceans will form.

13.5 Water Monitoring
Water monitoring would evaluate the stability of water systems and their interaction with the atmosphere and surface.

The project would assess freezing, evaporation, water availability and changes in environmental conditions.

The gravity-control field is assumed to be adjustable, but its effectiveness in improving water retention remains unresolved.

13.6 Water Stabilization Milestone
The milestone for Stage A2 is the establishment of suitable, persistent water conditions in selected regions.

The completion of this stage would not necessarily mean that Mars has a global water cycle or extensive oceans.

It would indicate that the selected regions have progressed sufficiently to support the next stages of biological preparation.

14. Stage A3 — Preparation of Growing Regions
Plant preparation is intended to begin when selected locations can be developed toward suitable biological conditions.

The project does not assume that the entire planet must be environmentally suitable before preparation begins. Selected locations could be assessed and prepared separately, provided their environmental conditions meet the relevant requirements.

14.1 Selection of Growing Locations
The project would identify regions suitable for biological preparation.

The selection would depend on environmental conditions, water availability, temperature, pressure, surface characteristics and the availability of appropriate growing materials.

The proposal does not establish specific locations or validated environmental thresholds.

14.2 Growing Substrate
Plants require more than water.

The project would need to establish suitable growing substrates that provide the physical and chemical conditions needed for the selected organisms.

This includes consideration of substrate composition, nutrient availability, moisture retention and other biological requirements.

The proposal does not assume that untreated Martian surface material is automatically suitable for terrestrial plants.

14.3 Nutrient Availability
The project would assess the availability of essential nutrients for selected plants.

Water and environmental stability alone are not sufficient to guarantee plant growth.

The growing regions would require suitable nutrient conditions and appropriate monitoring.

14.4 Environmental Conditions
The project would evaluate whether selected growing regions have suitable:

Temperature.

Pressure.

Water availability.

Light.

Atmospheric composition.

Nutrients.

Substrate conditions.

Environmental stability.

The requirements would depend on the organisms selected.

14.5 Organism Selection
The proposal assumes that suitable plants or starter organisms would be selected for the intended environmental conditions.

The project would not assume that all terrestrial plants can survive in the resulting Martian environment.

Organism selection would depend on biological tolerances and the environmental conditions established in the growing regions.

14.6 Protected Growing Environments
Where necessary, the project could use protected or controlled growing environments during early biological development.

The purpose would be to establish conditions suitable for the selected organisms while monitoring their response.

This stage would prepare the growing locations for initial biological introduction.

15. Stage A4 — Initial Plant Introduction and Observable Growth
The first biological milestone in Scenario A is observable growth in selected, monitored locations.

This milestone represents the beginning of biological development within the project's prepared growing regions.

It is not a claim that Mars has become globally habitable.

15.1 Initial Introduction
The introduction of selected organisms would be conditional on the environmental and biological requirements being met.

15.3 First Observable Growth Milestone

It would not establish:

Global vegetation.

Earth-like temperatures.

Stable global oceans.

A self-sustaining planetary ecosystem.

The milestone is specifically intended to represent initial biological growth.

16. Stage A5 — Continued Environmental and Biological Development Through Year 300
The remaining portion of the Scenario A development period is intended for continued environmental and biological development.

The project assumes that the first 150 years following delivery provide a broad planning window during which environmental conditions, water systems and biological development can continue to be evaluated.

The exact duration of individual activities remains unresolved.

16.1 Expansion of Suitable Growing Areas
The project could continue evaluating and preparing additional growing locations as environmental conditions permit.

The expansion of biological areas would depend on the success of initial growth and the environmental conditions of the new locations.

16.2 Continued Water Development
Water systems would continue to be monitored.

The project would evaluate whether water availability, stability and distribution are improving or changing over time.

The development of extensive water systems remains conditional on the physical water inventory and environmental conditions.

16.3 Continued Biological Development
The project would evaluate the survival, growth and reproduction of introduced organisms where appropriate.

Additional biological development would depend on the success of earlier stages and on suitable environmental conditions.

16.4 Atmospheric Monitoring
Atmospheric conditions would continue to be monitored throughout the development period.

The project would evaluate whether atmospheric changes are persistent, whether environmental conditions are improving in selected regions, and whether the hypothetical gravity-control field is associated with the intended effects.

16.5 Ecological Development
The proposal aims toward broader ecological development.

However, the introduction of plants does not automatically establish a self-sustaining ecosystem.

Further ecological development would require appropriate organisms, environmental conditions, interactions between biological systems and continued monitoring.

16.6 Year 300 Review Milestone
Year 300 is the overall Scenario A planning milestone.

At that point, the project would conduct a comprehensive review of the environmental and biological development achieved during the proposed 300-year planning period.

Scenario A's 150-year post-delivery period is therefore a broad development allowance. It is not a precise prediction of when plants will first grow or when Mars will become habitable.

26. Key Difference Between the Scenarios
Scenario A — Extended development
Provides up to 150 years after delivery for environmental development, water stabilization, plant preparation, initial growth and continued ecological progress.

Scenario B — Accelerated development
Aims for initial observable plant growth in 10–18 months after delivery, conditional on the hypothetical gravity-control field, advance preparation and environmental requirements.

Scenario A provides a broader development window and does not rely on the same extreme post-delivery acceleration assumption.

Scenario B aims to shorten the initial development period dramatically. Its success depends more heavily on the hypothetical gravity-control technology, sufficient material retention and the ability to prepare the necessary infrastructure and biological systems before delivery.

Both scenarios share the same transportation concept, the same hypothetical mass-reduction assumption and the same broad objective of improving Mars's environmental conditions.

Neither scenario guarantees a globally habitable Mars by its stated endpoint.

PART 8 — FINAL PROJECT OBJECTIVE AND CONCLUSION
32. Overall Project Objective
The EUROPA-TO-MARS TRANSFORMATION PROJECT proposes relocating Europa from its original orbital environment around Jupiter to Mars over a stipulated 150-year transportation period.

The project assumes the development of hypothetical technologies that would reduce Europa's effective mass during transportation and influence Mars's effective gravity following delivery.

The revised delivery concept assumes a collision-based introduction of Europa's physical material inventory into the Martian environment. The intended contribution includes water-bearing material, ice, minerals, volatile compounds and other physical resources.

The project then aims to develop environmental conditions more favorable to persistent water and initial plant growth.

The long-term objective is to work toward a Mars with more stable atmospheric conditions, persistent water in selected regions, initial biological growth and the potential for broader ecological development.

This objective is speculative and depends on technological, physical, environmental and biological assumptions that have not been demonstrated.

33. Scenario A — Extended Development
Scenario A assumes:

150 years for Europa's proposed transportation and delivery.

An additional 150 years of post-delivery development.

An earlier working estimate of 20–50 years for atmospheric and surface stabilization.

An earlier working estimate of 30–100 years for water accumulation and stabilization.

Plant preparation, introduction and initial growth when suitable environmental conditions have been established.

Continued development and monitoring through the overall Year 300 planning milestone.

Approximately 300 years is the project's planning horizon, not a guarantee of planetary transformation.

34. Scenario B — Accelerated Development
Scenario B assumes:

150 years for Europa's proposed transportation and delivery.

6–9 months for atmospheric and surface stabilization.

3–6 months for initial water stabilization.

1–3 months for plant preparation, introduction and initial observable growth.

The intended post-delivery milestone is initial observable plant growth within 10–18 months.

Approximately 150 years plus 10–18 months is a conditional planning target, not a scientifically validated forecast.

35. Essential Project Sequence
The overall conceptual sequence is:

Establish the hypothetical Europa mass-reduction assumption.

Establish the proposed transportation framework.

Begin Europa's relocation from the Jupiter region.

Account for the transportation environment, including the asteroid-belt region, within the transportation assessment.

Continue the proposed transportation period toward Mars.

Complete the defined delivery event.

Assess the post-delivery environment.

Evaluate the retained material and environmental conditions.

Evaluate the hypothetical Mars gravity-control system.

Develop selected regions toward suitable atmospheric and surface conditions.

Establish persistent water where conditions permit.

Prepare growing environments.

Introduce suitable organisms when their requirements are met.

Measure and verify initial growth.

Continue environmental and biological development.

Evaluate long-term environmental stability and ecological progress.

The collision-based delivery revision introduces an additional uncertainty into both scenarios. Neither timeline establishes that the collision will produce the intended environmental outcome or that the subsequent stages will proceed within their proposed intervals.

The project must therefore distinguish between its assumed technologies, calculated arithmetic, proposed milestones, unresolved scientific questions and actual measured outcomes.

The final goal in both scenarios is the development of more favorable Martian environmental conditions, persistent water in selected regions, initial plant life and the possibility of broader ecological development over time. Neither scenario guarantees that Mars becomes globally habitable or self-sustaining.
